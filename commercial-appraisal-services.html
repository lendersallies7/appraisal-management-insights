Commercial appraisal services, explained

A commercial appraisal is not a bigger residential appraisal. It is a written argument for value, built from up to three independent methods, and reconciled by an appraiser who has to explain their reasoning.

Commercial appraisal services cover the valuation of income-producing and non-residential real estate for lending, portfolio management, financial reporting and litigation support. For most banks and credit unions, the driver is credit: an appraisal supporting a loan secured by commercial property.

Unlike residential work, there is no standard form. The appraiser defines a scope appropriate to the specific property and assignment, then produces a narrative report — frequently eighty to two hundred pages — setting out the analysis and the conclusion.

What gets appraised

Commercial appraisal covers a wide range of asset classes, and competency in one does not imply competency in another. The categories that behave most differently:

Office
Multi-tenant and single-tenant. Lease structure, tenant credit quality and remaining term drive value as much as the building does.
Retail
From single units to anchored centres. Location, trade area and tenant mix matter more than construction quality.
Industrial
Warehouse, distribution, light manufacturing. Clear height, loading and access frequently outweigh square footage.
Multifamily
Apartment buildings above the residential threshold. Income analysis dominates, with a deep pool of comparable sales in most markets.
Hospitality
Hotels and motels. Operating businesses as much as real estate, requiring analysis of performance data rather than rent.
Special purpose
Churches, schools, medical, self-storage, land. Limited comparable evidence, so the cost approach often carries real weight.

The three approaches, and which one matters

An appraiser may develop up to three independent approaches to value. Which ones are developed, and how heavily each is relied upon, depends on the property and the available evidence.

Sales comparison

What similar properties have sold for, adjusted for differences. Straightforward in principle, constrained in practice by how few genuinely comparable commercial sales occur in any given market.

Income capitalisation

What the property earns, converted into a capital value. Requires analysis of rent rolls, lease abstracts, expense ratios, vacancy and market rent, and the selection of a capitalisation or discount rate the appraiser can defend. For most income-producing property this is the primary approach.

Cost

What it would cost to replace the improvements, less depreciation, plus land value. Most informative for new construction and for special-purpose property where comparable sales barely exist.

Defining the assignment

More commercial appraisals go wrong at the engagement stage than at any other point, because the assignment itself was imprecise.

Three things need settling before an appraiser starts:

The value premise. As-is, as-stabilised, or as-complete. These produce different numbers on the same property, and a report delivering one when the credit decision needed another is a wasted assignment.

The interest being valued. Fee simple, leased fee, or leasehold. On a tenanted building these diverge substantially, because a below-market lease reduces the value of the landlord's position without changing the building.

The intended use and user. Stated in the report and limiting. An appraisal prepared for one institution is not automatically usable by another.

Who is qualified to do the work

Commercial transactions above $500,000 require a state certified appraiser, and all transactions of $1,000,000 or more require a certified appraiser. At or below $500,000, a federally related commercial transaction may be supported by an evaluation instead of an appraisal.

Certified general appraisers — those qualified for commercial work without limitation — are a considerably smaller population than residential appraisers, and their numbers have been declining. In secondary and tertiary markets, the number competent in a specific asset class can be very small.

Competency is asset-class specific, not just geographic. An appraiser strong on industrial work is not automatically qualified on hospitality, and the standards require them to decline or disclose where they are not.

Why technical review matters more here

A residential appraisal is largely a completed dataset. A commercial appraisal is an argument, and arguments benefit from a second qualified reader.

A technical review examines whether the scope was appropriate, whether each approach was applied correctly, whether the capitalisation rate is supportable, whether the market analysis reflects reality, and whether the conclusion follows from the evidence presented.

On higher-exposure assignments this is where problems surface — usually not errors, but assumptions that looked reasonable in isolation and less so once examined. Institutions that order review only when something already seems wrong are using it as a remedy rather than a control.

What to provide, and when

Commercial timelines are driven more by documentation than by appraiser availability. The items most often supplied late:

  • Rent roll, current and dated
  • Operating statements, two to three years
  • Leases or lease abstracts, including options and escalations
  • Capital expenditure history and planned works
  • Site survey, floor plans and building dimensions
  • Property tax bills and any assessment appeals
  • Environmental reports, where relevant
  • Details of any tenant in default or renegotiation

A file where these arrive in the first week finishes materially earlier than one where they arrive in instalments. This is the part of a commercial appraisal timeline a lender genuinely controls.

Common questions

How long does a commercial appraisal take?

Three to six weeks is typical, with complex or unusual assets taking longer. The largest variable is documentation. The second is appraiser availability in the specific market and asset class.

Why is it so much more expensive than residential?

The work is different in kind. A narrative report requires scope definition, market and submarket research, lease analysis, and up to three separate approaches to value, each of which must be developed and explained.

Can an evaluation be used instead?

For federally related commercial transactions at or below $500,000, yes. An evaluation need not be prepared by a licensed appraiser and need not comply with USPAP, but must remain independent of loan production and contain sufficient analysis to support the decision.

What is a capitalisation rate?

The rate used to convert a property's net operating income into a capital value. Selecting one is a judgement supported by market evidence, and it is frequently the single most consequential number in a commercial appraisal.

Does the appraiser need to see the leases?

For income-producing property, generally yes. Rent alone does not describe a lease — term, options, escalations, and who pays which expenses all affect value.
